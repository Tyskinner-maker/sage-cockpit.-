"We're building our own system"
Make them feel the real cost before they commit
›
Usually means: They think their business is too unique for off-the-shelf, have IT capacity sitting idle, or someone in leadership has a "we build everything ourselves" mentality. Don't argue against building — make them scope it out loud.
Rung 1 — genuine curiosity, make them think
"That's interesting — has your team scoped out what it would actually take to build and maintain it long term, or is it still in early conversation?"
This question does most of the work. Most teams haven't thought it through. Let the silence sit after you ask it.
Rung 2 — if they say they've scoped it
"Totally fair. The teams I've talked to that went that route — the build usually takes longer and costs more than the original estimate, and then they end up owning a maintenance problem forever. What's your plan for keeping it compliant as regulations change?"
Compliance is the killer question. ASC 606, multi-entity tax, audit trails — none of that is free to maintain in a custom build.
Rung 3 — if they're still committed
"I respect that — you know your business better than I do. The only thing I'd ask is would you be open to a 20-minute look at Intacct before you break ground? If it doesn't fit, at least you're building with full information."
Pain points worth naming if they engage
  • 18–36 months minimum before it's usable — Intacct is live in weeks
  • The team that builds it becomes a permanent dependency — what if they leave?
  • Compliance updates, security patches, audit requirements never stop — that's ongoing cost forever
  • Most internal builds never fully finish — scope creep kills them before go-live
  • No support, no SLA, no roadmap — you own every problem that comes up
  • 30,000 companies already solved this problem — you'd be rebuilding from scratch what we've refined for 20 years

"We prefer on-premise — everyone's in one place"
Not about cloud vs. on-premise — about what it's costing them
›
Usually: This is about control and familiarity, not actually about where the server lives. They've normalized the hidden costs and haven't thought through the edge cases. Don't argue cloud vs. on-premise — flip it to what on-premise is costing them right now.
Lead with this — acknowledge first
"That makes sense — and I'm not here to tell you how your team should work. Cloud doesn't mean your people leave the office, it just means your system isn't a bottleneck when they can't be there. My only question is what happens when your server needs an upgrade, or someone needs to pull a number at 9pm before a board meeting, or you open a second location — does on-premise make that easier or harder?"
If they push back further — flip to hidden cost
"Totally fair. I'm just curious — who owns the server maintenance on your end? Because with on-premise, someone's managing upgrades, downtime, and IT support every time there's a change. With Intacct it updates automatically every quarter, no IT project, no downtime. Most teams don't realize how much time that's actually costing them until they add it up."
If they bring up data security
"Actually that's one of the biggest misconceptions — on-premise means your financial data lives on a server in your building. If that server fails, gets hit with ransomware, or there's a flood or fire, what's your recovery plan? Cloud means enterprise-grade redundant backups and disaster recovery built in. Most mid-market on-premise setups don't have anything close to that."
Three angles — pick the one that fits
  • Growth / scale: "Works fine today — what happens when you add a location or someone needs access remotely?"
  • Hidden cost: "Who manages the upgrades and server maintenance — and how much time does that take?"
  • Security: "What's the disaster recovery plan if the server goes down or gets hit with ransomware?"

Mirror before you move

Name what you're hearing

Ask about impact, not just existence

React like a human first

Let silence work
Ask the question, then fully stop. Don't add "like for example" or "you know what I mean." The awkward pause is almost always them about to tell you something real.

Get discovery going. If they open up, you have a champion. If they don't, you still have more than you came in with.
If they engaged but aren't the decision maker
"I appreciate that — sounds like you'd know better than anyone where the friction is. Who on your team would be the right person to loop in if we wanted to take it further — is there a Controller or CFO above you?"
If they shut it down early
"Totally fair — who would be the best person for me to connect with on the finance or accounting side? Just a name so I'm not calling blind."
A name is always the win here. Even a first name. "Just ask for Sarah in finance" is worth the whole call.
If they won't give a name or refer you
"No worries at all — what's the best way to get in touch with whoever handles those decisions? Email or phone usually better?"
Never ask "can I send you some info?"
If they're not the buyer they won't forward it and it gives you nothing. Always go for the name or the direct path instead.

Casual accounting specifics
Low pressure — sounds like curiosity, not qualification
›
These feel like small talk but tell you a lot. Use them to keep conversation flowing when someone is cautious or when you want to learn more without making it feel like an interrogation.
Team size — normalizes the conversation
"How big is your accounting team right now — are you a small group or do you have a bigger department?"
Small team + complexity = strong signal. 1–3 people doing multi-entity work manually is one of the best fits for Intacct.
Payroll — easy opener, surfaces complexity
"Do you handle payroll in-house or is that outsourced?"
If in-house — how are they running it and does it connect to their accounting system. If outsourced — still tells you about their stack and whether they're thinking about integration.
Who they use — normalizes their situation
"A lot of teams your size are still on QuickBooks or something similar — is that where you are, or are you on something else?"
Sounds like you know the space. Makes it easier for them to just confirm rather than feeling like they're being evaluated.
Audit — surfaces compliance pain naturally
"Do you go through an audit every year — internal or external?"
If yes — how painful is it, and how much manual prep goes into it. Audit prep is one of the most time-consuming things for a lean finance team and Intacct solves it directly.
Year-end — timing and pain in one
"How's year-end looking for you — is that coming up or are you just past it?"
Great for timing. Just past it = pain is fresh. Coming up = urgency is real. Either way it opens a natural conversation about close and reporting.
How they report up — surfaces visibility gap
"When your leadership team needs a financial update — how does that usually get to them? Do they pull it themselves or does your team put it together?"
"We put it together" = manual reporting = your in. Almost always lands.

Green light signals. Use to prioritize accounts and personalize your opener.

System signals
  • Using QuickBooks, Xero, or MS Dynamics (especially GP — it's sunsetting)
  • Multiple QB instances — one per entity or location
  • Finance team mentions Excel for reporting or consolidation
  • On McLeod with no separate accounting system or using QB alongside it
  • Recently outgrew a system and switched — may switch again
  • On NetSuite but complaining about cost, consultants, or complexity
Growth signals
  • Recent funding round (Series A/B, PE investment)
  • Acquired a company or opened new locations
  • New CFO, Controller, or VP Finance hired in last 6–12 months
  • Rapid headcount growth — finance team likely stretched
  • New product line, revenue stream, or business unit added
Structure signals
  • Multi-entity or multi-location business
  • PE-backed (needs investor-grade reporting)
  • Project-based or services business (complex revenue recognition)
  • Manufacturing, logistics, or field services (inventory + job costing)
  • Transportation / trucking company — likely on McLeod or similar TMS
Process signals
  • Close takes more than 5–7 business days
  • Consolidation done manually or in spreadsheets
  • No automated approval workflows
  • Finance team can't get real-time visibility without running a report
  • Compliance, audit, or board reporting pressure mentioned

Trucking & Logistics
Multi-entity, job costing, cash flow visibility
›
What they deal with
  • Multiple entities or terminals — consolidation done manually or not at all
  • Cash flow is everything — fuel costs, driver pay, maintenance hit fast and they need real-time visibility
  • Job costing per load or route is manual — hard to see which lanes or customers are actually profitable
  • Often family-owned, on QuickBooks or basic systems, new generation pushing for modernization
Opener angle
"We work with a lot of trucking and logistics companies where the finance team is managing multiple terminals or entities and still pulling everything together manually at close. I was calling to see if that sounds familiar — what are you on for accounting?"
Best discovery question
"How are you tracking profitability by lane, customer, or driver — is that in your accounting system or does someone build that separately?"

Manufacturing
Inventory, job costing, multi-location
›
What they deal with
  • Inventory costs and job costing are complex — hard to see true margin per product or order
  • Multiple plants or locations with separate books — consolidation is a monthly nightmare
  • AP volume is high — lots of vendors, purchase orders, and manual matching
  • Reporting to ownership or PE investors who want clean numbers fast
  • Often on QuickBooks or MS Dynamics (especially GP) — both hit hard ceilings as complexity grows
Opener angle
"We work with a lot of manufacturers who are managing multiple locations and finding that the accounting side hasn't kept up with the complexity of the operation. Calling to see if that's familiar — what are you currently using for accounting?"
Best discovery question
"How are you tracking cost per job or per product line — is that coming out of your accounting system or does someone build that in a spreadsheet?"
Dynamics GP angle
Manufacturing often runs GP. Lead with the sunsetting angle — "Microsoft announced they're ending support for GP. A lot of manufacturers are using this as the trigger to finally move to something built for the cloud."

Restaurants & Food Service
Multi-location, POS, food cost, franchise
›
What they deal with
  • Multiple QB instances — one per location, impossible to roll up cleanly
  • POS data doesn't flow into accounting — daily sales reconciled manually
  • Food cost and labor tracking done outside the accounting system
  • No visibility into which locations are profitable vs. dragging without building a report
  • Franchise groups dealing with royalty tracking in spreadsheets
  • PE-backed groups need investor reporting that R365 or QB can't produce cleanly
Opener angle
"We work with a lot of restaurant groups where the finance team is managing multiple locations and spending way too much time just getting the numbers together — close is slow, POS data isn't integrated, and visibility across locations is always a manual pull. Does any of that sound familiar — what are you on for accounting?"
Best discovery question
"How are you getting visibility across all your locations — is that a dashboard you can pull up or does someone have to build that report?"
R365 angle
Restaurant groups often land on R365. Respect it — frame Intacct as what runs the business that owns the restaurants. The gap is investor reporting, complex ownership structures, and multi-entity consolidation across non-restaurant entities.
